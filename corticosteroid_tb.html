Corticosteroid Adjunct Treatment in Tuberculosis

📚 Source: NTEP Guidelines (India)  |  INDEX-TB Guidelines (ICMR/CTD)  |  Standard Respiratory Textbooks (Fishman, Harrison, Crofton & Douglas)
📚  1. Introduction & Rationale
Core Principle: Corticosteroids do NOT kill Mycobacterium tuberculosis. They are purely adjuncts used to suppress the host's pathological immune response — preventing tissue destruction, reducing mortality, and limiting long-term morbidity — always alongside standard ATT.
  • Tuberculosis causes injury primarily through host-mediated immunopathology (granulomatous inflammation, cytokine storm, caseous necrosis, fibrosis) rather than direct bacterial toxicity alone.
  • This inflammatory cascade, if unchecked, causes irreversible organ damage: cerebral herniation in TBM, constrictive pericarditis, bronchial stenosis, adrenal destruction.
  • Glucocorticoids modulate this response without impairing mycobacterial clearance when adequate ATT is co-administered.
  • Indications are specific and limited — as per NTEP (National TB Elimination Programme) and INDEX-TB Guidelines (ICMR/Central TB Division, India).
  • Use outside defined indications carries risk of immunosuppression, steroid side-effects, and reactivation of latent infections.
⚠ Warning: Corticosteroids used in unrecognised or inadequately treated TB can precipitate fulminant disseminated disease. Always confirm adequate ATT coverage before or simultaneously with steroid initiation.
⚙️  2. Mechanism of Action of Corticosteroids in TB

The exact mechanism is incompletely understood, but the following pathways are well-established:

🔥
Anti-inflammatory Inhibit phospholipase A2 → reduced prostaglandins & leukotrienes. Suppress TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, interferon-γ.
🛡️
Immunosuppression Reduce T-lymphocyte proliferation and macrophage activation; dampen cytokine storm responsible for tissue damage.
💧
Anti-oedema Reduce vasogenic cerebral oedema (TBM), pericardial effusion formation, and pleural exudate volume.
🧬
Anti-fibrotic Reduce fibroblast proliferation → prevent constrictive pericarditis and meningeal adhesions causing hydrocephalus.
💊
Hormonal Replacement Bilateral adrenal destruction in TB → Addison's disease: physiological corticosteroid replacement is mandatory.
🧠
IRIS Modulation In HIV-TB, paradoxical IRIS driven by rapid immune recovery is suppressed by short-course prednisolone.
📋 Note (INDEX-TB): The benefit of steroids in TB is site-specific — the level of evidence is HIGH for TBM and MODERATE for pericardial TB; it is LOW or INSUFFICIENT for pleural, lymph node, and abdominal TB.
✅  3. Indications for Corticosteroids in TB (NTEP & INDEX-TB Guidelines)

3.1  Definite / High-Evidence Indications

  • TB Meningitis (TBM):
    • Strongest, most evidence-based indication — INDEX-TB: Strong recommendation, high-quality evidence.
    • Reduces mortality by ~25% and decreases severe neurological sequelae.
    • Reduces vasogenic cerebral oedema, meningeal inflammation, and adhesions causing hydrocephalus.
    • Indicated in all grades (I, II, III) of TBM; grade II and III benefit most.
    • In HIV-positive TBM: steroids may be used if no other life-threatening OIs present (INDEX-TB: Conditional recommendation, very low quality evidence).
  • Tuberculous Pericarditis:
    • INDEX-TB: Conditional recommendation, moderate quality evidence.
    • Reduces risk of constrictive pericarditis and accelerates resolution of pericardial effusion.
    • Reduces all-cause mortality in HIV-negative patients.
    • Evidence of mortality benefit in HIV-positive patients is less clear (IMPI Trial data).

3.2  Conditional / Selective Indications

  • Adrenal TB (Addison's Disease): Bilateral adrenal destruction leads to primary adrenal insufficiency. Physiological corticosteroid replacement (hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone) is mandatory and lifelong if permanent damage.
  • TB-IRIS (Paradoxical IRIS): In HIV-TB co-infected patients starting ART — moderate to severe paradoxical reactions (worsening lymphadenopathy, CNS IRIS, severe systemic symptoms) benefit from short-course prednisolone. Continue both ART and ATT.
  • Endobronchial TB (especially children): To prevent progressive bronchial stenosis/obstruction; reduces inflammatory granulation tissue.
  • Severe Miliary TB with ARDS: Selected cases with life-threatening systemic inflammatory response; individualised use under close monitoring.
  • Drug Hypersensitivity to ATT: Short course for severe skin reactions (e.g., SJS/TEN) while planning drug re-challenge or alternative regimens.
  • Laryngeal TB with Airway Obstruction: Short course to reduce mucosal oedema and prevent airway compromise.

3.3  NOT Routinely Recommended (INDEX-TB)

✘ INDEX-TB explicitly states: Corticosteroids are NOT recommended as routine adjuncts for:
  • Pleural TB — no proven long-term benefit on pleural fibrosis or lung function.
  • Lymph node TB — insufficient evidence.
  • Abdominal / Peritoneal TB — no benefit demonstrated.
  • Spinal TB (Pott's disease) — not indicated unless cord oedema is compressing the spinal cord (rare, individualised).
💊  4. Drugs, Doses & Duration (NTEP / INDEX-TB)
TBM — Adults
  • Drug: Dexamethasone (preferred) or Prednisolone
  • Dose: 0.4 mg/kg/day IV initially
  • Phase 1: 7 days IV at full dose
  • Taper: Reduce over 6–8 weeks (IV → oral)
  • Or: Prednisolone 60 mg/day oral, tapering weekly
  • Total duration: 6–8 weeks
TBM — Children (NTEP Paediatric Guidelines)
  • Drug: Dexamethasone / Prednisolone
  • Dexamethasone: 0.6 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks
  • Taper: Over subsequent 4 weeks
  • Or: Prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day (max 60 mg)
  • Total duration: 8 weeks
Tuberculous Pericarditis
  • Drug: Prednisolone
  • Adult dose: 60 mg/day (or 1 mg/kg/day)
  • Week 1–2: 60 mg/day
  • Taper: Reduce by 10 mg/week
  • Total duration: 6–8 weeks
TB-IRIS
TB-IRIS (HIV-TB Co-infection)
  • Drug: Prednisolone
  • Week 1–2: 1.5 mg/kg/day
  • Week 3–4: 0.75 mg/kg/day
  • Total duration: 4 weeks
  • Continue both ART and ATT throughout
Adrenal TB — Addison's Disease
  • Hydrocortisone: 20 mg morning + 10 mg evening
  • Fludrocortisone: 0.1 mg/day (mineralocorticoid replacement)
  • Duration: Lifelong if permanent adrenal damage
  • Sick-day rules: double dose during illness/surgery
Endobronchial TB (Paediatric)
  • Drug: Prednisolone
  • Dose: 1–2 mg/kg/day (max 40 mg)
  • Duration: 6–8 weeks with taper
  • Aim: prevent bronchial stenosis
Severe Miliary TB / TB-ARDS
  • Drug: Methylprednisolone / Prednisolone
  • Dose: Individualised (0.5–1 mg/kg/day)
  • Duration: 2–4 weeks, then taper
  • Only in life-threatening inflammatory response
  • Under specialist supervision

🧠  5. Management Algorithm: TB Meningitis with Corticosteroids
📌 NTEP Key Point: For TBM, the anti-TB regimen duration is extended to 12 months total (2HRZE + 10HR) as per NTEP guidelines, which is longer than standard pulmonary TB (6 months). Corticosteroids are used only for the initial 6–8 weeks of this regimen.

📋 Even in Grade III TBM, corticosteroids are recommended as they may still reduce mortality, though the absolute benefit is smaller than in Grade I/II. The Thwaites et al. (Lancet, 2004) landmark trial demonstrated survival benefit of dexamethasone in Vietnamese adults with TBM — this forms the basis of global and Indian recommendations.
⚠️  7. Contraindications & Cautions

7.1 Absolute / Relative Contraindications to Corticosteroids in TB

✘ Uncontrolled Diabetes Mellitus Steroids cause severe hyperglycaemia; strict glucose monitoring and insulin adjustment mandatory if steroids cannot be avoided.
✘ Active Peptic Ulcer Disease Risk of GI bleed; co-prescribe PPI (proton pump inhibitor) if steroids essential.
✘ Severe Osteoporosis Long-course steroids worsen bone loss; calcium + Vitamin D supplementation needed.
✘ Active Psychosis / Severe Psychiatric Illness Steroids can precipitate or worsen psychosis.
✘ Concurrent Untreated Fungal Infections Immunosuppression may disseminate fungal infections (e.g., candidiasis, aspergillosis).
✘ MDR-TB / XDR-TB (Relative) Use with great caution — bactericidal cover may be suboptimal; only use if clear-cut indication (e.g., TBM) and adequate drug regimen is in place.

7.2 Special Caution: HIV-Positive Patients

  • Higher risk of opportunistic infections (CMV, PCP, cryptococcal meningitis) with steroid use.
  • Rule out cryptococcal meningitis before starting steroids for presumed TBM in HIV patients — CrAg (cryptococcal antigen) test is mandatory.
  • INDEX-TB: In HIV+ TBM — steroids are a conditional recommendation with very low quality evidence; clinical judgement required.
  • Monitor CD4 count and viral load closely during steroid course.

7.3 Rifampicin Drug Interaction (Critical)

⚠ Important Drug Interaction: Rifampicin is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 — it accelerates metabolism of corticosteroids (especially prednisolone) by 45–70%. This means:
  • Standard prednisolone doses may be sub-therapeutic when given with rifampicin.
  • Some guidelines suggest doubling the prednisolone dose or using dexamethasone (less affected by rifampicin induction).
  • Dexamethasone is the preferred steroid for TBM partly because it is less subject to this interaction.
  • Monitor clinical response carefully; dose upward titration may be required.

🔍  9. Monitoring Protocol During Corticosteroid Therapy in TB

Before Starting Corticosteroids

  • Confirm adequate ATT has been started (or start simultaneously).
  • Fasting blood glucose and HbA1c — screen for DM.
  • Blood pressure, weight, electrolytes (Na, K), renal function tests.
  • In HIV+ patients: CD4 count, CrAg (cryptococcal antigen), viral load.
  • Chest X-ray and baseline ophthalmology review (if anticipated long course).
  • Rule out strongyloidiasis in endemic areas (Ivermectin prophylaxis may be considered).

During Corticosteroid Therapy

1
Weekly (First Month): Blood glucose, blood pressure, weight, serum electrolytes. Clinical neurological assessment in TBM (GCS, focal deficits).
2
Every 2 Weeks: Assess for signs of secondary infection — fever pattern, new symptoms. Hepatic function tests (steroid + ATT drug interaction monitoring).
3
Monthly: Full blood count, renal function, LFTs, lipid profile (if on prolonged steroids). In pericardial TB: echocardiography to assess effusion resolution and early constriction.
4
In TBM specifically: Monitor GCS, cranial nerve palsies, repeat neuroimaging if clinical deterioration. Watch for paradoxical worsening (IRIS-like response after steroid taper).
5
During Taper: Gradual dose reduction — do NOT stop abruptly (risk of adrenal crisis). Educate patient on sick-day rules. Watch for rebound inflammation.
6
After Stopping Steroids: Continue ATT to full course completion. Reassess HPA axis if course was >3–4 weeks. ACTH stimulation test if prolonged course or clinical features of adrenal insufficiency post-taper.

❤️  10. Management Algorithm: Tuberculous Pericarditis with Corticosteroids
📌 INDEX-TB on Pericardial TB: The IMPI trial (2014) showed that prednisolone reduced constrictive pericarditis (NNT = 11) in HIV-negative patients but showed no significant mortality benefit in HIV-positive patients. Hence, steroid use in HIV-positive pericardial TB remains a conditional, individualised decision as per INDEX-TB guidelines.
📌  11. Special Situations & Practical Considerations

11.1 Corticosteroids in Paediatric TB

  • Indicated in TBM, pericardial TB, and endobronchial TB in children.
  • Endobronchial TB with segmental/lobar collapse — prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day for 6–8 weeks prevents progressive stenosis.
  • In miliary TB with respiratory failure in infants — short-course steroids may be life-saving.
  • NTEP Paediatric Module: Dexamethasone preferred for TBM in children; careful growth monitoring during and after steroid course.
  • Avoid prolonged steroid use without clear indication — growth retardation and HPA axis suppression are major concerns.

11.2 Corticosteroids in Drug-Resistant TB (DR-TB)

  • Steroids may be used in DR-TB with TBM or pericardial involvement — the indication is the site of disease, not the drug sensitivity pattern.
  • However, exercise greater caution in MDR-TB as bactericidal activity of second-line drugs is lower — adequate drug coverage must be assured.
  • No evidence supports routine steroid use in uncomplicated MDR-TB.
  • NTEP DR-TB Module: Follow same site-specific indications as drug-susceptible TB.

11.3 Paradoxical Worsening (TB-IRIS) — Steroid Protocol

  • Definition: Clinical deterioration after starting ART in HIV-TB patients due to rapid immune reconstitution — "unmasking" or "paradoxical" IRIS.
  • Features: Enlarging lymph nodes, worsening respiratory symptoms, new CNS involvement, high fever.
  • Management (INDEX-TB / NTEP):
    1. Do NOT stop ART or ATT.
    2. NSAIDs for mild IRIS.
    3. Prednisolone 1.5 mg/kg/day for 2 weeks → 0.75 mg/kg/day for 2 weeks (total 4 weeks) for moderate-severe IRIS.
    4. In CNS-IRIS: Dexamethasone preferred; neurosurgical input if hydrocephalus.

11.4 Steroid Taper — Why Gradual?

⚠ Abrupt cessation of corticosteroids after a course longer than 3 weeks risks adrenal crisis (hypotension, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte imbalance) due to HPA axis suppression. Taper reduces this risk. Typical taper:
  • Prednisolone: Reduce by 10 mg every 1–2 weeks from full dose.
  • Dexamethasone: Stepwise reduction over 4–6 weeks.
  • Below 10 mg prednisolone: Reduce by 1–2 mg every 2 weeks (most critical phase).

High-Yield Exam Points (Most Commonly Asked)

  • ★ TBM is the NUMBER ONE indication — always mention first in any exam answer.
  • ★ Dexamethasone is preferred over prednisolone in TBM — less CYP3A4 induction by rifampicin.
  • ★ Rifampicin reduces prednisolone levels by 45–70% — critical pharmacokinetic interaction.
  • ★ NEVER use steroids without ATT — can cause fulminant TB dissemination.
  • ★ INDEX-TB explicitly does NOT recommend steroids for pleural TB — common exam trap.
  • ★ In HIV + TBM — always rule out cryptococcal meningitis (CrAg test) before steroids.
  • ★ TBM ATT duration = 12 months (NTEP) — longer than standard pulmonary TB (6 months).
  • ★ IRIS steroids — do NOT stop ART or ATT; prednisolone 1.5 mg/kg × 2 weeks.
  • ★ Adrenal TB = lifelong hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone replacement if permanent damage.
  • ★ Pericardial TB in HIV-negative — steroids reduce constrictive pericarditis (NNT = 11, IMPI trial).
📚 NTEP 2023 Guideline Summary on Steroids: Corticosteroids are recommended adjuncts in TB meningitis (strong), tuberculous pericarditis (conditional), and adrenal TB (mandatory replacement). Their use in pleural TB, lymph node TB, and abdominal TB is not supported by sufficient evidence and is not recommended as routine practice in the Indian national programme.
