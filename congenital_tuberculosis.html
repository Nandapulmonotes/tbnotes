CONGENITAL TUBERCULOSIS
10-Mark Structured Answer  |  Based on Standard Respiratory Textbooks & NTEP Guidelines

1. Definition

Congenital Tuberculosis (CTB) is a rare but potentially fatal form of tuberculosis in which the newborn acquires Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection:
  • In utero (transplacentally via umbilical vein), OR
  • During passage through an infected birth canal (intrapartum), OR
  • By aspiration/ingestion of infected amniotic fluid
Key distinction: Differs from postnatally acquired neonatal TB where infection occurs after birth via respiratory droplets.

2. Epidemiology

  • Extremely rare — approximately <400 cases reported in world literature (up to recent decades)
  • India bears a high burden — TB in women of reproductive age increases risk
  • Maternal genital TB (endometrial TB) is a significant predisposing factor
  • Mortality without treatment: >50%; with treatment: reduced significantly if diagnosed early
  • Overall incidence in infants born to mothers with active TB: ~0.5–1%
  • Risk is higher in HIV-positive mothers with concurrent TB

Pathogenetic Steps (Haematogenous Route — Most Common)

  • Step 1: Mycobacteria reach placenta via maternal bacteraemia → placental tuberculous lesion forms
  • Step 2: Bacilli enter fetal circulation via umbilical vein
  • Step 3: First pass through fetal liver → hepatic primary complex (Ghon focus in liver)
  • Step 4: Bacilli reach lung, spleen, and other organs via systemic circulation
  • Step 5: Granuloma formation, caseation, and dissemination in immunologically immature neonate
  • Fetal immune system is relatively Th2-skewed → impaired granuloma containment → rapid dissemination

4. Diagnostic Criteria

4a. Historical: Beitzki's Criteria (1935)

TB lesions must be present in the infant AND a primary complex must be demonstrated in the liver (indicating haematogenous spread from placenta).

4b. Current Standard: Cantwell's Revised Criteria (1994)

The infant must have proven tuberculous lesions (culture/histology/molecular) AND at least ONE of the following:
① Lesions present in the first week of life
② Primary hepatic complex or caseating hepatic granulomas on biopsy/autopsy
③ Proven TB infection of placenta or maternal genital tract
④ Postnatal transmission excluded by thorough contact investigation (including hospital attendants)
Note (NTEP/WHO): If postnatal TB cannot be excluded AND no primary hepatic complex, the case is classified as neonatal TB rather than congenital TB. The distinction has therapeutic implications.

Onset of Symptoms

  • Signs may appear as early as Day 1–2 of life (haematogenous route) or as late as 2–3 weeks (aspiration route)
  • Average age at presentation: 2–3 weeks postnatal
  • Prematurity is common in affected neonates

6. Investigations

Microbiological

  • Gastric aspirate: AFB smear + culture (gold standard for pulmonary CTB)
  • CBNAAT / Xpert MTB-RIF (NTEP preferred): Rapid molecular test on gastric aspirate, tracheal aspirate, BAL, CSF, hepatic aspirate — highly sensitive; also detects rifampicin resistance
  • Culture: MGIT liquid culture (superior to LJ medium); takes 2–6 weeks
  • AFB smear: Low sensitivity in neonates; NTEP now recommends CBNAAT over smear as first-line
  • Placental histology & culture: Mandatory — cornerstone of Cantwell criterion ③

Radiological

  • Chest X-ray: Miliary pattern, alveolar consolidation, mediastinal lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion
  • HRCT Chest: Miliary nodules, tree-in-bud pattern, hilar adenopathy
  • USG Abdomen: Hepatosplenomegaly, intra-abdominal lymphadenopathy, peritoneal thickening
  • MRI Brain: TB meningitis, tuberculomas (if CNS involvement suspected)

Other Investigations

  • Liver biopsy / Hepatic aspirate: Caseating granulomas confirm hepatic primary complex
  • CSF analysis: Lymphocytic pleocytosis, raised protein, low glucose — if CNS signs present
  • TST (Mantoux): Usually negative in neonates — immature cell-mediated immunity; unreliable
  • IGRA: Also unreliable in neonates — not recommended by NTEP for this age group
  • CBC: Anaemia, leucocytosis or leucopenia; thrombocytopenia may occur
  • LFTs: Elevated in hepatic involvement
  • Maternal workup: Sputum AFB/CBNAAT, CXR, genital TB evaluation (endometrial biopsy)

9. Treatment

Principle: Treat as Disseminated / Severe TB (equivalent to TB meningitis category in terms of drug selection and duration) — as per NTEP & WHO guidelines for paediatric TB.

9a. Drug Regimen (NTEP 2024 Paediatric Guidelines)

Phase Duration Drugs Remarks
Intensive Phase 2 months Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E) 4-drug regimen; add Ethambutol (100 mg/day) as per NTEP 2024
Continuation Phase 7–10 months Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) Total duration: 9–12 months for disseminated/CNS disease

9c. Additional Measures

  • Corticosteroids: Prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day for 4–8 weeks if TB meningitis or severe hypoxia
  • Drug-Susceptibility Testing (DST): Mandatory for all neonatal/congenital TB cases (NTEP universal DST — U-DST policy)
  • MDR-TB protocol: If rifampicin resistance detected on CBNAAT → refer to DR-TB centre; newer drugs (Bedaquiline, Delamanid) considered in consultation with paediatric TB experts
  • Supportive care: Respiratory support (O2/ventilation if needed), nutrition, IV fluids
  • BCG vaccination: Delay BCG until treatment is complete and infant is stable

10. Management of the Mother & Contacts

  • Mother should receive full anti-TB treatment if not already on therapy
  • Breastfeeding: Can be continued once mother is on treatment and non-infectious (>2 weeks of therapy); first-line anti-TB drugs are safe in breast milk in small amounts
  • Separation of mother and infant: Only if mother is smear-positive and in early treatment; avoid prolonged separation
  • All household contacts must be screened for TB
  • Infant should receive isoniazid preventive therapy (IPT) if exposed to MDR-TB source

12. Prevention (NTEP Framework)

  • Screening all pregnant women for TB symptoms at every antenatal visit — as per NTEP Nikshay protocol
  • CBNAAT testing of pregnant women with TB symptoms — rapid diagnosis reduces vertical transmission risk
  • Prompt treatment of TB in pregnant women — reduces risk of congenital TB to near zero if treated adequately before delivery
  • Genital TB screening in women with infertility or recurrent pregnancy loss — early detection prevents transmission
  • Nikshay Poshan Yojana — nutritional support for TB patients including pregnant women (NTEP)
  • BCG vaccination of neonate — protects against disseminated TB and TB meningitis (not against congenital TB per se, as infection is prenatal)
  • Contact tracing — all household contacts of smear-positive mothers must be screened
  • Isoniazid Preventive Therapy (IPT):
    • Given to neonates born to mothers with active pulmonary TB (smear-positive)
    • Isoniazid 10 mg/kg/day for 6 months (NTEP 2024)
    • TST/IGRA at 6 months — if positive, complete full anti-TB treatment
    • If negative at 6 months, stop IPT and administer BCG

14. NTEP 2024 — Key Highlights for Congenital TB

  • Universal DST (U-DST): All confirmed TB cases including neonates must have DST performed — CBNAAT is the first-line test
  • Nikshay registration: All TB cases including paediatric and congenital must be registered on the Nikshay portal
  • Ni-kshay Mitra: Community volunteers support TB patients — includes nutritional and social support for families
  • CBNAAT over AFB smear: NTEP 2024 recommends CBNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF or Xpert Ultra) as the primary diagnostic tool — higher sensitivity in paucibacillary disease such as CTB
  • Pyridoxine supplementation: Mandatory with INH in all neonates and infants (NTEP 2024 Paediatric TB Guidelines)
  • Daily fixed-dose combinations (FDC): Weight-band based paediatric FDCs recommended; available at NTEP drug stores
  • Bedaquiline / Delamanid: Approved for use in paediatric DR-TB under NTEP — consult DR-TB expert committee for neonatal cases
  • TB Preventive Treatment (TPT): Isoniazid monotherapy (6H) for household infant contacts of smear-positive TB — mandatory under NTEP
  • End TB strategy: India's target — zero TB deaths by 2025 (TB-Mukt Bharat); congenital TB prevention is part of maternal TB elimination agenda
