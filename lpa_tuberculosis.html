Line Probe Assay (LPA) in Tuberculosis Diagnosis

FL-LPA (GenoType MTBDRplus) & SL-LPA (GenoType MTBDRsl) — NTEP India

10 MARKS | STRUCTURED ANSWER

📌1. Introduction & Definition

  • Line Probe Assay (LPA) is a WHO-endorsed, NTEP-recommended rapid molecular test for detection of Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex (MTBC) and drug resistance mutations directly from clinical specimens or culture isolates.
  • It is a hybridisation-based NAAT (Nucleic Acid Amplification Test) using biotinylated primers and nitrocellulose paper strips bearing specific DNA probes.
  • Two types are in clinical use under NTEP India:
    • FL-LPA (First-Line LPA) — GenoType MTBDRplus V2 → detects resistance to Rifampicin (R) & Isoniazid (H)
    • SL-LPA (Second-Line LPA) — GenoType MTBDRsl V2 → detects resistance to Fluoroquinolones (FQ) & Second-Line Injectable Drugs (SLIDs)
  • Both are manufactured by Hain Lifescience, Germany and are the only WHO-pre-qualified LPA tests.

NTEP Position: Under India's Revised NTEP/PMDT guidelines, LPA (FL & SL) is placed at Intermediate Reference Laboratories (IRLs) and selected designated microscopy centres (DMCs). It is the standard of care for rapid MDR-TB and Pre-XDR-TB diagnosis prior to liquid culture DST.

⚗️2. Principle of Line Probe Assay

Basic Molecular Mechanism

  • Based on Reverse Hybridisation technology combined with PCR amplification.
  • Sample DNA is extracted → PCR-amplified using biotinylated primers targeting resistance-determining gene regions.
  • Amplicons (PCR products) are then denatured and hybridised to membrane-bound oligonucleotide probes on a nitrocellulose strip.
  • Probe-amplicon binding → colorimetric reaction via streptavidin–alkaline phosphatase system → visible coloured bands on the strip.

Two Types of Probes

🟢 Wild Type (WT) Probes

  • Target normal (unmutated) gene sequences
  • Band present = gene is normal (sensitive)
  • Band absent = mutation likely → resistance inferred
  • Cover the full susceptibility region of each gene

🔴 Mutant (MUT) Probes

  • Target specific known mutation sequences
  • Band present = specific mutation detected → resistance confirmed
  • Band absent = that specific mutation not present
  • Identify clinically significant resistance mutations

NTEP India — Indications for FL-LPA

  • All presumptive RR-TB / MDR-TB patients (as upfront test before liquid culture DST)
  • Direct testing from AFB smear-positive sputum (sensitivity comparable to culture isolate)
  • Indirect testing from culture isolates (any smear status)
  • HIV-positive patients, paediatric TB, EPTB (selected specimens)
  • Not recommended for smear-negative specimens (low sensitivity)

SL-LPA V2 Advantage: Version 2.0 includes eis promoter probes which improves kanamycin resistance detection sensitivity from ~50% (V1) to ~96% (V2). This is critical for Pre-XDR-TB diagnosis under NTEP.

NTEP India — Indications for SL-LPA

  • All confirmed RR-TB / MDR-TB patients before initiating second-line treatment
  • Can be performed directly on smear-positive AND smear-negative sputum (unlike FL-LPA)
  • Also on MDR-TB confirmed culture isolates (indirect testing)
  • Used to exclude FQ and SLID resistance before starting the shorter MDR-TB regimen
  • Detection of Pre-XDR-TB (MDR + FQ resistance) and XDR-TB patterns
  • Not reliable for extrapulmonary specimens (low sensitivity)

Important NTEP Note — Pre-XDR-TB Definition (Updated 2021): WHO and NTEP revised the XDR-TB definition in 2021. Pre-XDR-TB = MDR/RR-TB + resistance to any FQ. XDR-TB = MDR/RR-TB + FQ resistance + resistance to at least one of Bedaquiline or Linezolid. SL-LPA is the primary diagnostic tool to classify patients into these categories under NTEP.

✅8. Advantages & Limitations of LPA

✅ Advantages

  • Rapid TAT (24–48 hrs) vs 6 weeks for culture DST — crucial for early MDR-TB treatment
  • High sensitivity and specificity (especially for rifampicin)
  • Detects both presence and identity of specific mutations
  • Simultaneous detection of MTB complex + drug resistance in one test
  • High throughput — up to 48 samples per run, cost-effective at scale
  • Only BSL-2 facility needed (unlike culture DST requiring BSL-3)
  • SL-LPA V2 detects eis promoter mutations, improving low-level kanamycin detection
  • Guides decision on shorter MDR-TB regimen eligibility (NTEP)
  • Reduces time to effective treatment, improving outcomes and limiting transmission
  • Validated for direct testing on clinical specimens (FL-LPA on smear-positive sputum)

❌ Limitations

  • FL-LPA not reliable on smear-negative sputum or EPTB specimens
  • Detects only known mutations — novel/rare mutations causing resistance may be missed
  • Cannot detect resistance to Ethambutol (embB), Pyrazinamide, or newer drugs (Bdq, Lzd, Cfz)
  • SL-LPA V2 has lower sensitivity for capreomycin compared to amikacin/kanamycin
  • False resistance: heteroresistance may cause partial or faint bands — difficult to interpret
  • Requires dedicated lab with PCR infrastructure and trained personnel
  • Cannot determine MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration) — phenotypic DST needed for confirmation
  • Does not detect pe/ppe gene mutations causing some INH resistance phenotypes
  • Cannot differentiate viable from non-viable bacilli (unlike culture)
  • Contamination risk if pre-PCR and post-PCR areas are not separated

🛡️9. Quality Control & Biosafety in LPA (NTEP Standards)

Internal Controls on Each Strip

Control Band Purpose Expected Result If Absent — Action
Conjugate Control (CC) Confirms conjugate & colorimetric detection reagents working Always Present Discard; repeat entire run
Amplification Control (AC) Confirms DNA extraction & PCR amplification successful Always Present Repeat DNA extraction; check inhibitors
TUB Band Confirms MTBC-specific amplicon present Present if MTBC detected If absent but AC present → NTM / non-MTBC

External Quality Assurance (EQA) under NTEP

  • Blinded panel testing — NRL supplies known control strains quarterly to all IRLs
  • On-site evaluation — Annual supervisory visits by NRL to IRL performing LPA
  • Proficiency testing — Minimum acceptable performance: ≥90% concordance with reference results
  • All LPA results must be entered into Ni-kshay portal (NTEP digital surveillance platform)
  • Discordant results between LPA and phenotypic DST must be reported to NRL for investigation

Biosafety Requirements

  • Processing of sputum for DNA extraction — BSL-2 facility with biosafety cabinet (Class II)
  • All post-PCR steps can be performed at BSL-2 open bench with strict zone separation
  • Separate pre-PCR, PCR, and post-PCR areas mandatory to prevent amplicon contamination
  • All liquid waste treated with 1% hypochlorite before disposal

🚀10. Newer Developments & Future Perspective

  • GenoType MTBDR Plus V3 (in evaluation): Includes additional probes for embB (Ethambutol resistance) and pncA (Pyrazinamide) — expands drug coverage significantly.
  • Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS): Emerging as a comprehensive resistance profiling tool; NTEP has initiated pilot WGS programmes at select NRLs to supplement and eventually replace LPA in high-burden settings.
  • Targeted Next-Generation Sequencing (tNGS): Platforms like Deeplex-MycTB can detect resistance to 13+ drugs simultaneously from direct sputum — being validated for programmatic use.
  • Truenat MTB-RIF Dx: Chip-based real-time PCR available at peripheral levels; complements but does not replace LPA for comprehensive resistance profiling.
  • Despite newer tools, LPA (FL & SL) remains the operational backbone of NTEP's rapid DST strategy for the foreseeable future due to proven reliability, throughput, and cost-effectiveness.

NTEP 2024 Strategic Position: Under the National Strategic Plan for TB Elimination 2017–2025 and PMDT guidelines, LPA (FL + SL) testing is mandated for 100% of RR-TB/MDR-TB patients before treatment initiation. India has scaled LPA capacity to >80 functional IRLs nationwide, processing over 1 lakh LPA tests annually, making it the world's largest programmatic LPA deployment.

🎯12. Conclusion

  • Line Probe Assay (FL-LPA and SL-LPA) represents a paradigm shift in TB diagnostics — enabling same-week treatment decisions for MDR-TB and Pre-XDR-TB, replacing the traditional 6–8 week wait for culture DST.
  • Under India's NTEP, the sequential use of FL-LPA followed by SL-LPA forms the cornerstone of Programmatic Management of Drug-Resistant TB (PMDT).
  • FL-LPA detects MDR-TB by identifying rpoB (rifampicin) and katG/inhA (isoniazid) mutations; SL-LPA then classifies patients into MDR / Pre-XDR / XDR-TB by detecting gyrA/gyrB and rrs/eis mutations.
  • Despite minor limitations (smear positivity requirement for FL-LPA, inability to detect novel mutations), LPA remains the most operationally feasible, rapid, validated molecular DST tool at the IRL level in India's TB elimination programme.
  • Its role will continue alongside emerging technologies (WGS, tNGS) as India marches toward the TB-Free India goal by 2025 and the End TB Strategy target of 2030.

Examiner's Takeaway: LPA = Rapid molecular DST by reverse hybridisation. FL-LPA (rpoB + katG + inhA) → MDR-TB diagnosis. SL-LPA (gyrA + gyrB + rrs + eis) → Pre-XDR/XDR-TB classification. Both placed at IRL level under NTEP. TAT 24–48 hrs vs 6 weeks for culture. Mandatory for all RR-TB patients in India before treatment initiation.
