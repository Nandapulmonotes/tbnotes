Miliary Tuberculosis

Respiratory Medicine · Long Answer · Indian Standard Textbooks & NTEP Guidelines
10 MARKS

1. Definition

Miliary TB (MTB) is a life-threatening form of TB resulting from massive haematogenous dissemination of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), producing uniform, discrete, millet-seed–sized (1–2 mm) nodules diffusely scattered throughout the lungs and other viscera.

Disseminated TB (DTB) refers to concurrent involvement of at least two non-contiguous organ sites, or involvement of blood / bone marrow by TB. (Sharma & Mohan; NTEP 2023)

2. Epidemiology

  • Accounts for <2% of all TB cases in clinical studies; up to 20% in autopsy series
  • India remains a high-burden country — NTEP 2023 estimates ~2.8 million TB cases/year
  • Age shift: Classically children; now also adolescents, young adults, and elderly (cryptic form)
  • Gender: Males predominate; recent series show female preponderance owing to better healthcare access
  • Risk factors driving resurgence: HIV/AIDS pandemic, widespread immunosuppressants, TNF-α inhibitors, diabetes mellitus
  • BCG vaccination has substantially reduced MTB and TB meningitis in children

3. Pathogenesis

Primary / Post-primary TB Focus (Lung or Extrapulmonary)
Caseous Necrosis → Erosion of Blood / Lymphatic Vessel Wall
(Weigert's Focus — intimal caseous vasculitis)
Massive Lymphohaematogenous Dissemination of Mtb
Embolisation to Vascular Beds of Multiple Organs
Uniform 1–2 mm Granulomatous Foci — "Miliary Pattern"

Key Immunopathology

  • Th1 response (CD4+ T cells, IFN-γ, IL-12) → granuloma formation → protective
  • Th2 predominance in MTB → inhibits granuloma → favours dissemination
  • Non-reactive MTB: No granulomas; necrotic foci teeming with bacilli → seen in severe immunosuppression (ARDS, septic shock picture)
  • HIV → ↓ CD4+ cells → ↓ Th1 → ↑ risk of MTB, especially when CD4 <200/µL
  • IRIS: Paradoxical worsening in HIV patients started on HAART

4. Predisposing / Associated Conditions

Host Factors

  • HIV / AIDS (CD4 <200)
  • Malnutrition
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Chronic renal failure / dialysis
  • Underlying malignancy
  • Silicosis
  • Pregnancy / postpartum
  • Extremes of age (infants, elderly)

Iatrogenic / Drug-Related

  • Corticosteroids
  • Immunosuppressants / cytotoxics
  • TNF-α inhibitors (infliximab, etanercept)
  • Intravesical BCG therapy (bladder Ca)
  • Organ transplantation
  • Extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy
  • Cardiac valve homograft replacement
  • Post-gastrectomy

6. Diagnosis (NTEP 2023 / Sharma & Mohan)

Diagnostic Criteria for MTB

  • (i) Clinical presentation: fever with evening rise, weight loss, anorexia, tachycardia, night sweats >6 weeks, responding to ATT
  • (ii) Classical miliary pattern on chest radiograph
  • (iii) Bilateral diffuse reticulonodular lesions on CXR or HRCT
  • (iv) Microbiological / histopathological evidence of TB

Diagnostic Algorithm

Clinical Suspicion: Prolonged fever + constitutional symptoms + risk factors

Radiology Summary

  • CXR (PA): 1–2 mm uniform, discrete, pinpoint opacities — "snowstorm" appearance; sensitivity ~60–70%, specificity 97–100%
  • HRCT chest: Superior — shows miliary nodules, interlobular septal thickening, ground-glass opacities, tree-in-bud pattern, lymphadenopathy; nodules 2–3 mm
  • CECT abdomen: Hypodense lesions in liver / spleen, peritoneal/omental nodules, psoas abscess, intra-abdominal lymphadenopathy
  • MRI brain: Multiple hyperintense enhancing disk-like lesions (tuberculomas); mandatory if CNS involvement suspected
  • PET-CT / PET-MRI: Defines disease extent in DTB/MTB; assesses activity of residual lesions post-treatment

Laboratory Findings

Haematological

  • Normocytic normochromic anaemia (~50%)
  • Leucocytosis or leucopenia
  • Thrombocytopenia / thrombocytosis
  • Leukaemoid reaction (rare)
  • Haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis
  • DIC in acute septicemic form
  • Elevated ESR, elevated CRP

Biochemical

  • Hyponatraemia (SIADH) — prognostic
  • Hypoalbuminaemia — poor prognosis
  • ↑ Alkaline phosphatase (83%)
  • ↑ Transaminases (42%)
  • Hyperbilirubinaemia
  • Hypercalcaemia (uncommon)
  • Hypoxaemia (PaO₂ <60) ~40%
TST / Tuberculin Skin Test: Negative in 20–74% of MTB patients (anergy). A negative TST never excludes MTB. IGRA also has limited sensitivity. TST often becomes positive after successful therapy.

8. Treatment (NTEP 2023 / WHO / ATS-CDC-IDSA)

Principle: Untreated MTB/DTB is uniformly fatal within one year. Do NOT delay treatment pending confirmation in a sick patient with high clinical suspicion.

Standard NTEP Drug Regimen (Drug-Susceptible MTB)

Intensive Phase — 2 months

  • Isoniazid (H)
  • Rifampicin (R)
  • Pyrazinamide (Z)
  • Ethambutol (E)

Daily FDC under DOTS

Continuation Phase

  • Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R)
  • 4 months — standard MTB (no meningitis)
  • 7–10 months — with TB meningitis
  • 7 months — with bone & joint TB

Adjunctive Corticosteroids

  • Absolute indication: Adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease from TB )
  • Strong recommendation: TB meningitis — Dexamethasone 0.4 mg/kg/day × 4 weeks tapering over 8 weeks
  • TB pericarditis: Prednisolone 60 mg/day tapering over 6–8 weeks (reduces effusion, prevents constriction)
  • MTB with ARDS / severe hypoxaemia: Prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day × 4–6 weeks
  • MTB with severe pleural effusion / peritonitis: Short-course corticosteroids may be used
  • Steroid dose for TBM (NTEP 2023): Dexamethasone 0.3–0.4 mg/kg/day IV × 4 weeks → oral taper × 4 weeks
Drug Susceptibility Testing (DST): MANDATORY in ALL MTB patients as per NTEP 2023. GeneXpert MTB/RIF (CBNAAT) is the first-line rapid test — detects Mtb & Rifampicin resistance simultaneously within 2 hours. If RIF-resistant → treat as RR-TB / MDR-TB per NTEP protocol.

9. Drug-Resistant MTB (NTEP 2023)

  • If GeneXpert detects Rifampicin resistance (RR-TB) → treat as MDR-TB until DST report available
  • Shorter BPaLM regimen (NTEP 2023 / WHO 2022): Bedaquiline + Pretomanid + Linezolid + Moxifloxacin × 6 months
  • Standard longer regimen: 6–8 months intensive (Bdq + Lfx/Mfx + Cs/Cfz + Z + Am/Km) + 12 months continuation
  • MTB with XDR-TB: individualized regimen, 18–20 months minimum
  • All RR/MDR-TB patients: DST to second-line drugs mandatory; baseline audiometry, ECG (QTc) monitoring

10. Complications

Pulmonary Complications

  • Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) — 3–11%
  • Acute respiratory failure / mechanical ventilation
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • Spontaneous pneumothorax
  • Pleural effusion / empyema
  • Bronchopneumonia / superinfection

Extrapulmonary Complications

  • TB meningitis / Tuberculoma
  • Pericarditis / Cardiac tamponade
  • TB peritonitis / Ileus
  • Hepatic failure (granulomatous hepatitis)
  • Addisonian crisis (adrenal TB)
  • Pancytopenia (bone marrow involvement)
  • DIC / Septic shock
  • Haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)
  • Multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS)
  • IRIS in HIV co-infection

12. Prognosis & Outcomes

  • Overall mortality: 15–30% in modern series (despite treatment); up to 60% in HIV co-infected patients
  • Untreated MTB: Near 100% fatality within 6–12 months — "pthisis florida"
  • Early initiation of ATT is the single most important factor determining survival

Poor Prognostic Indicators

  • Age >60 years

  • Cryptic form
  • ARDS / respiratory failure
  • Coma / altered sensorium

  • Hypoalbuminaemia (<2.5 g/dL)
  • Thrombocytopenia
  • Leucopenia or leukaemoid reaction
  • DIC
  • Severe hypoxaemia (PaO₂ <60)
  • TB meningitis coexistence
  • Multi-organ dysfunction
  • HLH complicating MTB
  • Drug-resistant MTB (RR/MDR/XDR)
  • Non-reactive histology
  • Persistence of miliary shadows >3 months

13. Key NTEP 2023 Highlights for MTB

NTEP (National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme) 2023 — India's roadmap to eliminate TB by 2025
  • Universal DST: All TB patients (including MTB) must undergo GeneXpert (CBNAAT) at diagnosis — available at all District TB centres
  • Nikshay portal: Mandatory notification of all TB cases including MTB within 24 hours — civil and private practitioners both obligated
  • Nikshay Poshan Yojana: ₹500/month nutritional support to all TB patients during treatment
  • FDC tablets: Fixed-dose combination tablets in 3 weight bands (adult) and 4 weight bands (paediatric) for accurate weight-based dosing
  • New shorter regimens: BPaLM / BPaL for MDR/XDR-TB patients; reduces treatment duration from 18–20 months to 6 months
  • Preventive therapy (TPT): 6H or 3HP (3 months weekly INH + Rifapentine) for household contacts of MTB patients and PLHIV
  • Expanded diagnostic network: CBNAAT at block level, NAAT platforms (TrueNat) at PHC level under NTEP 2023
  • Target: 90% reduction in TB incidence and 95% reduction in TB deaths by 2025 (India's National Strategic Plan)

14. High-Yield Memory Aids

Causes of Miliary TB — "IMPAIRED"

  • I — Immunosuppression (HIV, steroids)
  • M — Malignancy
  • P — Post-primary reactivation
  • A — Age extremes (infants, elderly)
  • I — Intravesical BCG therapy
  • R — Renal / hepatic failure
  • E — End-stage organ disease
  • D — Diabetes mellitus

Investigations — "CLUE-BAL"

  • C — Choroidal tubercles (fundoscopy)
  • L — Liver biopsy (highest histopathological yield)
  • U — Urine AFB
  • E — ECHO (pericardial TB)
  • B — Bone marrow biopsy
  • A — ARDS work-up (BAL + TBLB)
  • L — Lumbar puncture (CSF for TBM)
Examiner's Favourite Points:
  • Choroidal tubercles = only pathognomonic sign of MTB
  • Cryptic MTB = older patients, no fever, normal TST, normal CXR — diagnosed at autopsy
  • TST negative in >50% MTB — NEVER exclude MTB on basis of negative TST
  • TB meningitis extends continuation phase to 7–10 months
  • HIV + MTB → start ATT first, then HAART within 2 weeks if CD4 <50
  • Rifampicin + Cyclosporin → markedly ↓ cyclosporin levels → dose adjustment
  • NTEP 2023 mandates universal DST and CBNAAT as first-line test
