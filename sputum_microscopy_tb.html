Sputum Microscopy in Tuberculosis Diagnosis
Pulmonary Infections · NTEP-Aligned · Examination Answer
10 Marks ~1100 Words NTEP 2021 / TOG 2016 High Yield

1. Introduction

  • Sputum smear microscopy is the cornerstone of TB diagnosis in India and resource-limited settings — simple, rapid, inexpensive, and highly specific
  • Remains the primary bacteriological tool for diagnosis of pulmonary TB in the programmatic setting under NTEP
  • Detects Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) and other mycobacteria as Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) based on the property of acid-fastness — retention of carbol fuchsin dye despite decolourisation with acid-alcohol
  • Acid-fastness due to high mycolic acid content of the mycobacterial cell wall — forms a waxy lipid-rich barrier
  • Requires approximately 5,000–10,000 bacilli/mL of sputum for positivity (lower sensitivity than culture)

2. Sputum Collection — NTEP Protocol

A. Specimen Type

  • Early morning sputum (EMS) — best quality, highest bacillary load
  • Spot sputum — collected at the time of visit to the health facility
  • NTEP currently recommends two sputum specimens: one spot + one early morning
  • Minimum volume: 3–5 mL of mucopurulent material

B. Ideal Sputum Characteristics

  • Mucopurulent or blood-tinged — not saliva or post-nasal drip
  • Collected in wide-mouthed, leak-proof, labeled container
  • Patient instructed to take deep breath and cough forcefully from chest
  • Collected in open air or well-ventilated area to minimize nosocomial transmission

C. Special Situations

  • Non-expectorating patients: chest physiotherapy, nebulized hypertonic saline (sputum induction)
  • Paediatric patients: gastric lavage aspirate (early morning, 3 consecutive days) or nasopharyngeal aspirate
  • Extrapulmonary TB: appropriate specimens — CSF, pleural fluid, lymph node aspirate, urine (renal TB)

Steps of ZN Staining:

1. Smear preparation → heat fix → flood with carbol fuchsin, steam heat 3–5 min

2. Decolourise with 3% acid-alcohol (30–60 sec) → wash with water

3. Counterstain with methylene blue or malachite green (2 min) → wash → dry

4. Examine under oil immersion (×100 objective) — read minimum 100 fields

✦ AFB appear: slender, beaded, slightly curved rods, 1–4 µm

7. Limitations of Sputum Microscopy

📉 Analytical Limitations

  • Cannot distinguish MTB from NTM — both appear AFB positive
  • Cannot provide Drug Sensitivity Testing (DST)
  • Requires high bacillary load (~10,000/mL) — low sensitivity in early disease
  • Smear-negative TB: sputum-scarce HIV-TB, paediatric TB, EPTB, cavitating disease healing
  • Morphologically viable and dead bacilli both stain positive (cannot assess viability)

⚙️ Operational Limitations

  • Quality depends on smear preparation and staining technique
  • Reader skill variability — external quality assurance (EQA) mandatory
  • Poor quality sputum (saliva) gives false negatives
  • Decolorization time critically affects result
  • High HIV-TB coinfection burden → higher smear-negative rates in India
NTM Pitfall: Non-tuberculous mycobacteria (MAC, M. kansasii) are AFB positive on ZN. Positive smear ≠ TB diagnosis — clinical, radiological, and molecular correlation is essential per NTEP guidelines.

8. Quality Assurance in NTEP Microscopy Network

  • EQA (External Quality Assurance): Panel testing, blinded rechecking, and on-site evaluation — quarterly at DMC level
  • Blinded Rechecking: 10% of all slides (both positive and negative) rechecked by supervisory laboratory
  • Lot Quality Assurance Sampling (LQAS): Used in some settings to assess laboratory performance
  • Designated Microscopy Centres (DMCs): NTEP-accredited labs with standardised SOP, trained staff, maintained equipment
  • Panel testing: Known positive/negative panels sent to DMCs to assess sensitivity/specificity
  • Error rate monitoring: Major errors (>2%) trigger re-training and corrective action

9. Role of Follow-up Smear Microscopy in Treatment Monitoring

NTEP DS-TB Regimen: 2HRZE (Intensive Phase) / 4HRE (Continuation Phase). Smear follow-up at end of IP is no longer mandatory for all patients per updated NTEP guidelines — clinical response and weight gain are key outcome parameters. However, smear at end of IP is done in sputum-positive patients to assess bacteriological response.
Monitoring Point Action
End of Intensive Phase (2 months) Smear in baseline smear-positive patients; if positive → extend IP or reassess for DR-TB
End of Treatment (6 months) Smear + culture if available → Treatment Outcome assessment
Treatment Outcome: Cure Completed treatment + bacteriological confirmation of cure (smear/culture negative at end of treatment + at ≥1 earlier occasion)
Treatment Failure (smear) Smear positive at month 5 or later → send for DST, escalate to DR-TB pathway

10. Special Considerations

A. HIV-TB Coinfection

  • Smear-negative TB is disproportionately common in PLHIV — 30–50% of HIV-TB may be smear negative
  • Due to reduced cavitation, lower bacillary load, atypical radiological picture
  • NTEP mandates CBNAAT (TrueNat/GeneXpert) as the primary test in PLHIV with presumptive TB — not smear alone
  • Smear negative result does not exclude TB in an HIV-positive patient

B. Paediatric TB

  • Paucibacillary disease — most children are smear negative
  • Gastric lavage AFB culture preferred over smear in children under 5
  • Diagnosis relies on clinical scoring (Stegen-Toledo criteria), Mantoux, CXR, contact history

C. Smear Positive, Culture Negative ("Dead Bacilli")

  • Can occur post-treatment — dead AFB stain positive but do not grow in culture
  • Does not necessarily indicate treatment failure — clinical context essential

✅ Examiner's Point: Though CBNAAT/TrueNat and culture have higher sensitivity, smear microscopy remains operationally indispensable in India's NTEP framework — scalable, low-cost, rapid, and deliverable at peripheral DMC level. It is the gatekeeper to the diagnostic cascade, not the final arbiter.
